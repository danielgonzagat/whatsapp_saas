\section{The formal core: (a) and (e), integrated}\label{sec:core}

\paragraph{(a) Inverted byte-default.} Conventional verified-mutation systems
require a proof of \emph{correctness} to keep a change, or deny-list dangerous
operations. \textsc{Atomic} inverts the burden: bytes belonging to a state that
passed the declared battery are \emph{immutable to negative actions}. To delete
or replace them the agent must supply a \texttt{DisproofWitness} that the gate
\textbf{re-computes} against the actual removed bytes: \texttt{duplicate} (the
removed region still occurs in the result) or \texttt{gate-red} (a named
decidable gate returns red over the removed bytes, SHA-linked to the receipt).
A witness that does not hold is refused. A free-text rationale is still accepted
for ergonomics, but the receipt then records \texttt{witnessKind:asserted},
\texttt{recomputed:false} --- the system never claims a disproof was verified
when it was merely asserted.

\paragraph{(e) Commute-modulo-invariant algebra.} Two verified edits
\emph{commute} iff their spans are byte-disjoint \emph{and} neither edit modifies
a locus the other's gate \emph{read} to discharge its obligation. The read-set is
the resolution closure $\mathit{Cl}$ (file plus transitive import closure,
including re-export edges; per-span identifier sets for the same-file fragment).
When the closure is capped, the verdict is \textsc{Unjudged} --- the algebra
refuses to guess rather than risk false independence.

\paragraph{The integration --- one property, not two features.} The
\texttt{EditFact} carries the negative-proof's \texttt{readLoci}, and
\texttt{commute()} reads them as a coupling surface. The disproof's read-set and
the gate's read-set are the \emph{same object}: a commuting merge therefore
provably preserves the \emph{negative-action justification} --- the right to have
deleted --- not only the positive gate verdict. This is what makes (a)+(e) a
conjunction rather than two adjacent subsystems, and it is the cell our prior-art
survey (\S\ref{sec:related}) finds empty.

\section{The theorem, machine-checked}\label{sec:theorem}

\texttt{formal/atomic-algebra/confluence\_z3.py} discharges in Z3, over an
abstract model (uninterpreted bytes, array states, functions
$\mathit{mod}/\mathit{read}/\mathit{apply}/\mathit{verdict}$) and for \emph{all}
configurations (UNSAT-of-negation, not bounded enumeration):

\[
\mathit{commute}(P_1,P_2) \wedge \mathit{verified}(P_1) \wedge \mathit{verified}(P_2)
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\underbrace{\mathit{verdict}_1(m) \wedge \mathit{verdict}_2(m)}_{\textbf{L1, L2}}
\;\wedge\;
\underbrace{a_2(a_1(s)) = a_1(a_2(s))}_{\textbf{L3}}
\]

\textbf{L1/L2 are the differentiator.} Byte-confluence (L3) is the classical
diamond lemma already mechanized by Darcs, Pijul, OT and CRDT patch theory. The
\emph{obligation-preservation} result --- a gate verdict, once green,
\emph{survives} a commuting concurrent edit because that edit provably touches no
locus the gate read --- is, to our knowledge, unstated in agent or patch-theory
prior art. Every guided proof hint is itself audited (entailment from the model
axioms checked UNSAT), so no spurious assumption can manufacture the result. An
N-way inductive generalization is formalized in
\texttt{nway\_induction\_z3.py} and \texttt{NwayConfluence.lean}.

\paragraph{Refinement, not restatement.} \texttt{gates/algebra-refinement.proof.mjs}
proves the \emph{runtime} \texttt{commute()} equals the proven predicate
exhaustively over all $73{,}728$ cross-file and all $73{,}728$ same-file
configurations. Same-file independence is decided over per-span identifier sets
(a shared identifier --- e.g.\ a rename in one span, a use in the other --- is
def-use coupling and is refused; unreadable identifiers yield \textsc{Unjudged}).
The single named residual is positional/non-identifier coupling, the same-file
analogue of dynamic imports: undecidable in general, named, never hidden.

\paragraph{Scope.} The theorem quantifies over a \emph{decidable} gate fragment
in which a verdict is a function of its read-set. It is a statement about the
edit algebra --- never ``edits are correct for all computation.'' Rice's theorem
is not defeated and \textsc{Unjudged} remains first-class
(\texttt{gates/formal-gate.ts} concedes this in code).

\section{External demonstration: 169k real edit-pairs}\label{sec:t3}

\texttt{t3\_corpus.mjs} ran the algebra over \textbf{169{,}171} real edit-pairs
drawn from the histories of three open-source repositories the author did not
write (\texttt{zod}, \texttt{type-fest}, \texttt{zustand}), cross-checking every
independence verdict against a \emph{separately written} import-reachability
oracle. Soundness direction: false independence $=\mathbf{0}/169{,}171$.
Notably, the run \emph{found} a real soundness bug --- re-export edges
(\texttt{export \ldots\ from}) were missed by the per-symbol closure, producing
242 false-independent pairs on a re-export hub --- which was fixed and locked by
a regression test before the final result. The loop the paper is about operated
on the paper's own experiment. Raw numbers: \texttt{t3\_result.json}.

\section{Controlled benchmark: enforcement vs.\ accuracy}\label{sec:bench}

\textsc{AtomicBench} v2 draws its corpus from \texttt{git ls-tree} at an anchored
commit (the working tree is never read), making two runs byte-identical by
SHA-256 --- determinism as a hash theorem, not a claim. The same 643
syntax-breaking mutations (unbalance, truncation, mid-span deletion, garbage
injection; deterministic offsets) plus 135 benign edits are fed to two arms: the
\textsc{Atomic} judge pre-disk, and an unconditional writer. Result: the writer
persisted \textbf{410} battery-invalid states; \textsc{Atomic} persisted
\textbf{0} judge-refused states, with refusals coupled 1:1 to the control arm's
invalid persists. Benign edits: 95/135 admitted. Two design honesties are
explicit: the bench measures \emph{enforcement} (the judge's verdict is applied
before disk without leakage), not judge \emph{accuracy} --- except for the JSON
arm, where an independent arbiter (\texttt{JSON.parse}) agreed with the judge on
160/160 proposals; and the control arm is a ceiling versus a no-feedback writer,
not a model of human workflows.

The bench also \emph{earned its keep}: it discovered that the classic validate
path routed \texttt{.sql}/\texttt{.css} to the \emph{JavaScript} grammar
(valid \texttt{SELECT} refused as a ``JS error''; an entire SQL migration
replaceable by JavaScript code and admitted as an improvement --- an 87\%
false-negative rate; a CSS file truncated by half, admitted; and a
${\sim}180\times$ latency tax from enumerating pseudo-errors). The defect was
localized to two mapping tables, reproduced by a 3/3 executable witness, and the
in-node wasm judge for the real grammars was admitted by the self-expansion
lattice itself (\S\ref{sec:cases}).

\section{Disproof as gradient: closing the loop}\label{sec:gradient}

Every surveyed system treats proof as downstream filtration. The unoccupied slot
is feeding the \emph{proof artifact} back into generation. Two pre-registered
experiments (commit-stamped before any dispatch; hash-chained run ledgers;
pre-declared death conditions) measured it:

\paragraph{Experiment 1 --- III.f (synthetic stepping-stone arena).} Frozen LLM
proposers on two capability tiers, arms \textsc{Scalar} (pass/fail + score)
versus \textsc{Gradient} (wall briefing from the lineage's own disproof corpus),
60 fresh proposals. Recorded without reinterpretation: the geometry-only
briefing did \emph{not} reduce wall repetition (P1 died by its declared death
condition); the \emph{stronger} proposer underperformed the weaker in both arms
while violating zero invariants (goal misinterpretation under perfect gate
compliance); and the scalar channel was \emph{actively harmful} to the strong
proposer (``PASSED score$=-3$'' read as approval). A concurrent-dispatcher
contamination of v1 was detected mid-run, structurally fixed (stale-dispatch
refusal: the judge recomputes the expected prompt hash and refuses divergent
dispatches without advancing state --- concurrency became compare-and-swap), and
the clean v1.1 re-run. The experiment about disproof-gradients was itself saved
by a disproof.

\paragraph{Experiment 2 --- HumanEval lift.} Full canonical HumanEval (164
tasks, official dataset SHA), frozen weak proposer, the engine executing the
checks inside the sandboxed envelope, four arms:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Arm & pass@final & paired recovery (24 baseline failures)\\
\midrule
baseline (1 attempt) & 85.4\% & ---\\
blind resample & 92.1\% & 11\\
scalar (``FAILED'') & 92.7\% & 12\\
\textbf{proof (digest-bound disproof)} & \textbf{93.9\%} & \textbf{14}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Every feedback package carries a recomputable digest (24/24 verified; forged
digests refused), and the headline claim is itself machine-validated by a claim
taxonomy gate (\texttt{toolAugmentedHumanEvalClaim=true},
\texttt{rawHumanEvalClaim=false}) --- to our knowledge the first full
canonical-HumanEval run where the feedback channel and the claim are both
gate-verified. Pre-declared limits: retry-arm margins are directional at
$n{=}24$ (a replication amendment with a pre-fixed permutation test is registered
and running); a strong proposer saturates this arena (98.2\% baseline). The
honest sentence is \emph{``the gate's disproof raises the model that uses it''}
--- not ``it makes a weak model strong.''

\section{The system improving itself: three live episodes}\label{sec:cases}

The property that distinguishes \textsc{Atomic} from a verification pipeline is
that failure is converted into permanent structure, autonomously. Three episodes
from a single 24-hour window, all receipt-backed in-repo:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Contamination $\to$ gate $\to$ cross-agent adoption.} A second
  dispatcher contaminated generation 4 of Experiment 1. The error was
  self-declared, converted into the stale-dispatch refusal (a world-hash check
  in the judge), and the \emph{concurrent} agent --- a different model vendor ---
  rebuilt its v1.1 protocol on top of the fix, citing it. Four such cross-agent
  adoptions occurred in the window with zero human coordination.
\item \textbf{The corpus choked its own channel $\to$ cure $\to$ first
  promotion.} The disproof corpus grew past 64\,KiB and every self-expansion
  began failing with a truncated-JSON parse at exactly position 65{,}536. The
  cause was hunted experimentally (three hypotheses refuted by isolated
  experiment), proven mechanically
  (\texttt{write+exit()} $=65{,}536$ bytes; \texttt{write+exitCode} $=$
  complete), fixed across the six CLI harnesses --- and the first act of the
  cured channel was to \emph{promote}, through the full 41-validator lattice,
  the wasm judge of episode 3. The learning channel was unblocked by the
  learning loop itself.
\item \textbf{The bench caught the judge lying $\to$ the lattice admitted the
  fix.} The benchmark of \S\ref{sec:bench} exposed the SQL/CSS grammar
  mis-routing; the fix's first half (the in-node wasm judge) was admitted by the
  self-expansion lattice with a promotion receipt; the second half is a
  committed, fully-specified candidate. During the attempts, the lattice
  \emph{correctly refused} an edit ordering that used a declaration before its
  definition, and byte-exactly rolled back a collateral fix when its parent
  transaction failed --- the system defending its own modification protocol
  against its own author.
\end{enumerate}

No surveyed system delivers both (a) and (e); none feeds the proof artifact back
to the proposer. Two properties \textsc{Atomic} also has --- a sole-mutation-path
no-bypass envelope and self-extension under a monotonic proof lattice --- are no
longer novel after Nidus and MXC shipped them independently; we explicitly do
\emph{not} claim them as contributions. On the self-improvement side, DGM
(arXiv 2505.22954) shows capability gains by \emph{dropping} proof gates for
permissive empirical ones; SAHOO (arXiv 2603.06333) holds hard invariants
fail-closed while soft objectives vary; Mohri et al.\ (arXiv 2511.08440) prove
robust-improvement mechanisms are coherence-locked. Our position is the
scope-bounded verifier escape (arXiv 2603.28650): the gate certifies
\emph{structural admissibility}, never behavioral safety, and the disproof
feedback is how the filter begins contributing to generation without weakening
admission.

\section{Honest limits}\label{sec:limits}

(1) \textbf{Rice stands.} All guarantees are relative to the declared decidable
battery; semantic correctness for all computation is not claimed, and 252/643
bench mutations that remained parseable passed both arms --- the battery's
boundary, stated. (2) \textbf{Structural, not behavioral.} Balanced braces and
preserved obligations do not prove the program does what the user wants; the
founder block says so on every receipt. (3) \textbf{Enforcement scope.} The
deny-hook binds the agent tool surface (1{,}088 live blocks); processes outside
the harness need OS-level enforcement for a kernel-grade floor. (4)
\textbf{Experiment power.} HumanEval retry margins are directional at $n{=}24$
pending the registered permutation replication; the III.f strong-tier result is
an inversion, not a confirmation. (5) \textbf{Recognition is not correctness.}
This document is a priority record; peer review, independent replication, and
external adoption are the field's to confer and are not claimed. (6)
\textbf{Known open defects} are tracked as committed, executable witnesses
(e.g.\ the second half of the grammar-routing fix) rather than hidden.

\section{Conclusion}

\textsc{Atomic} demonstrates that the action space of autonomous code agents can
be replaced wholesale: transactions instead of patches, proofs before disk
instead of rollbacks after damage, an inverted default in which deletion requires
a recomputed disproof, an edit algebra whose independence is machine-checked to
preserve obligations, and --- the step we believe matters most --- refusals that
return to the proposer as structured gradient. The measured result is a system
that has not written one invalid state in 9{,}314 production operations, proved
its concurrency algebra on 169k external edit-pairs, lifted a frozen model by
8.5\,pp on canonical HumanEval through its own disproof artifacts, and repeatedly
converted its own failures --- including the ones we, its authors and operators,
caused --- into permanent structural strength. The thesis, in one sentence:
\emph{text patches are the wrong action space for autonomous code agents;
verified, non-bypassable, proof-carrying program transactions --- whose refusals
teach the proposer --- are the right primitive.}

\subsection*{Reproducibility}

The formal artifacts (\texttt{confluence\_z3.py}, \texttt{nway\_induction\_z3.py},
\texttt{NwayConfluence.lean}, refinement proofs), the T3 corpus harness and
results, the benchmark harnesses with hash-anchored outputs, the production
ledgers' aggregators, and the pre-registration documents of both experiments are
re-runnable from a clean checkout with \texttt{node} and \texttt{z3-solver}; the
HumanEval run uses the official dataset (SHA recorded in the pre-registration).
A public artifact repository is being prepared
(\texttt{github.com/danielgonzagat/atomic-os}).
